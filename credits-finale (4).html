Freshman College Student Dies by Suicide After Struggling With Isolation and drugs

A freshman at CMTR University died by suicide last month after what her family says was a police investigation that moved too fast and asked too little.

Sophia Miller, 18, of downtown, was found dead on a factory nearby campus. The county medical examiner's office ruled the death a suicide within weeks, closing the case without further investigation.

Her mother, reached by phone, said she still doesn't understand what happened in the days before her daughter's death.

"She was doing okay. That's what everyone kept telling me — she was doing okay," her aunt said. "Nobody's told me anything since. It's like they decided what happened before they even looked."

Friends describe Sophia as bright, restless, and deeply loyal to a small circle of people close to her. She had recently attended a party on campus the weekend before her death — an event her family's attorney says investigators never fully accounted for in their initial report.

"There were witnesses at that party who were interviewed once, briefly, and never followed up with," said attorney [name], who is representing the family. "That's not an investigation. That's a formality."

The university, in a statement, said it could not comment on an individual student but pointed to its existing mental health resources.

"The wellbeing of our students is our highest priority, and a loss like this affects our entire community," the statement read. "We offer 24/7 crisis support, individual and group counseling, and are committed to supporting students however we can."

Youth suicide rates have risen nationally in recent years, according to CDC data, with mental health professionals citing isolation, academic pressure, and untreated substance use as contributing factors.

"These cases are almost never simple," said Dr clark , a licensed clinical psychologist not connected to the case. "When an investigation closes quickly, it's usually because of resources, not because there's nothing left to find."

The family says they've asked the District Attorney's office to formally reopen the case. As of this week, no decision has been made public.

If you or someone you know is struggling, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline is available 24/7 by calling or texting 988.
