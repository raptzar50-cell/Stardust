AKIRA [Studies photograph for approximately 90 seconds without speaking.]
AKIRA The lights come on before it's actually dark. Did you know that? In cities. The sensors trip early. So there's this window — maybe fifteen minutes — where it's not dark yet but all the lights are already on. Like the city is scared of the dark before the dark even arrives.
DR. ELLISON That's a very specific observation.
AKIRA I used to sit on the fire escape at my uncle's apartment. In Osaka. I was maybe eight. I would watch the lights come on. I thought the city was breathing. Inhale — dark. Exhale — light.
DR. ELLISON Do you miss Osaka?
AKIRA [Very long pause — over a minute. Picks up the photograph. Sets it down face-down.] I miss who I was when I was sitting on that fire escape. That's not the same thing as missing a place.
DR. ELLISON Who were you, then?
AKIRA Small. Quiet. Not in a broken way. Just — not yet filled in. Like a drawing that's all outline and no colour yet. I didn't know what was going to go inside all those lines.
DR. ELLISON And now? Do you know what goes inside the lines?
AKIRA I know some of it. [beat] I don't like all of it.
DR. ELLISON What part don't you like?
AKIRA [Turns photograph face-up again. Doesn't look at it.] The part where I stopped asking why things were the way they were. I used to ask all the time. My aunt said it was annoying. My teachers said it was disruptive. And then one day I just — stopped. Like a muscle you forget to use and then can't find anymore.
DR. ELLISON What made you stop?
AKIRA Something happened. I don't want to say it in a room like this.
DR. ELLISON That's okay. You don't have to say anything you're not ready to say.
AKIRA I know. [pause] That's actually the first useful thing anyone has said to me since I got here. [looks at Dr. Ellison directly for the first time in the session] The kid in the room next to the garden — the one who reads. Alex. Is he okay?
DR. ELLISON I can't discuss other patients.
AKIRA I know that too. [looks back at photograph] He was watching me draw on the window this morning. I could see his reflection. He thought I couldn't. I drew him a bird. I don't know if he saw it before it disappeared.
