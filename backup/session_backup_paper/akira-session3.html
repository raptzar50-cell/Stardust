DR. ELLISON I can't promise that, but I can promise I'll try not to make it feel like a thing.
AKIRA That's probably the most honest thing a doctor has ever said to me. [pause] Okay. I had a dream. About the school.
DR. ELLISON Which school?
AKIRA The one in ████████. The one before everything changed. In the dream it was empty. All the desks were there, all the books, the PE equipment in the hallways — everything exactly where it should be. But there were no people. Not one. And I was just walking through it, trying to find — I don't know what I was trying to find.
DR. ELLISON How did it feel? Walking through it?
AKIRA Familiar. That's what was strange. Not scary. Not sad. Just — like recognising a face you can't place. Like you know you know it, but you can't get to the name.
DR. ELLISON Did you find what you were looking for?
AKIRA I found a classroom at the end of a hallway I didn't recognise. It didn't feel like part of the school — the walls were a different colour. And there was someone sitting at the desk at the back. I couldn't see their face. But I knew — in the way you know things in dreams — that they had been waiting there for a very long time. Not for me. Just — waiting. And I woke up before I got close enough to see who it was.
DR. ELLISON Who do you think it was?
AKIRA [long pause — 43 seconds] I think it was me. A version of me that stayed. That didn't leave ████████. That made a different choice.
DR. ELLISON You say "choice." Do you feel the move — leaving ████████ — was a choice you made?
AKIRA [removes jacket, folds it in his lap] My mother called it a fresh start. My uncle called it practical. The school counsellor called it a transition. [pause] Nobody called it what it was.
DR. ELLISON What was it?
AKIRA Running. [beat] Which is fine. Sometimes you have to run. I just wish someone had said that word. I wish someone had said: we are running, Akira, and that's okay, but we are running. Instead of all those other words that meant the same thing but didn't feel honest.
DR. ELLISON What were you running from?

AKIRA [quietly, after the silence] I don't draw things on the window because I think anyone is watching. I draw them because the condensation is temporary. Whatever I put there, I know it's going to disappear. That's the whole point. I'm practicing something.
DR. ELLISON Practicing what?
AKIRA Letting go of things without having to decide to let go of them. The window just — takes them. I don't have to do anything.
DR. ELLISON [pause] That's a remarkable thing to have figured out, Akira.
AKIRA [small, unexpected smile] I drew a bird yesterday. Not for me. For the boy who reads.
DR. ELLISON Did he see it?
AKIRA I think so. He stayed at his window a long time after. [beat] I think some people need to be told that things disappear and it isn't a tragedy. I think he might be one of those people.
