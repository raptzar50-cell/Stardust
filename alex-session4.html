Pre-Session Note — Therapist

Alex arrived four minutes early today. He sat down before I had finished my notes from the previous session. He noticed. He said, "You always run late on Fridays." He was not wrong. Something is shifting. I am choosing not to name it yet in his presence.

Transcript — Verbatim

DR. ████ How's the book going? The one with the birds.
ALEX Finished it. It was okay. The ending was kind of a lie.
DR. ████ How do you mean, a lie?
ALEX The bird migrates back. Every year, like clockwork. The whole book is about how it always comes back. And then the last chapter — it just... doesn't. No explanation. It just doesn't come back that year. The author acts like that's a good ending.
DR. ████ And it isn't?
ALEX [pause — approx. 8 seconds] Some things should come back. That's all I'm saying.
DR. ████ Tell me about something that didn't come back, Alex.
ALEX [long pause] Are you going to write this down?
DR. ████ I take notes after. Not during. You know that by now.
ALEX My dad used to take me to this reservoir on Sunday mornings. Before school, before anything. Just to watch the water. He said the water remembered everything that had ever touched it. I used to think that was the stupidest thing I'd ever heard.
DR. ████ And now?
ALEX Now I think he might have been onto something. [beat] He stopped going. After the — [stops mid-sentence]
DR. ████ After the...?

ALEX Sorry. I don't know why I said that.
DR. ████ You don't have to apologise. You said exactly as much as you wanted to.
ALEX [quietly] Is that how this works? You just — wait until we say more than we meant to?

ALEX [pause] I think so. Yeah.

DR. ████ I can't discuss other patients.
ALEX I know. I just — he draws these things on the window. With his finger. In the condensation. And then he watches them disappear. Every morning. Same thing.
DR. ████ What does he draw?
ALEX I don't know. I can never tell from across the courtyard. But it makes me think about the reservoir again. About the water remembering things. [pause] I think we might have something in common. Me and him. I don't know what yet.

Post-Session Clinical Note

Significant progress in session four. Alex demonstrated voluntary disclosure for the first time — specifically referencing his father and a memory associated with loss and routine. He stopped himself before completing the account, which suggests the material remains unprocessed and emotionally active. The redacted section (by order of senior review) pertains to an allegation that requires legal verification before it can be included in the open record.

His unprompted observation about Patient 003 is notable. Connection between the two patients may be clinically relevant. Recommend monitoring for interpatient dynamics. Do not engineer contact — observe whether it develops organically.
