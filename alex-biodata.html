Presenting Concerns

Alex was referred to Eastbrook following a series of incidents at his secondary school that culminated in a mandatory juvenile court intervention in February 2024. According to intake notes, Alex has demonstrated persistent withdrawal from peer relationships, recurring episodes of what teachers describe as "emotional shutdown," and a documented pattern of self-isolating behaviour that predates his current school placement by approximately two years.

His guardian reports that Alex was a communicative and socially engaged child until the age of twelve, after which his demeanour shifted markedly. The precipitating event — if one exists — has not been disclosed by the family at this stage of intake. When asked directly, Alex responded with a single word: "Nothing."

Intake Clinician Note — Dr. Ellison

Patient presents as guarded but not hostile. Maintains eye contact intermittently. Responds to direct questions with minimal elaboration. Showed a brief but notable reaction when the name of his previous school was mentioned — recovered quickly and denied any significance. Flag for Session 1 follow-up. Do not press on family history until rapport is established.

Initial Observations

Alex arrived at Eastbrook carrying a single canvas bag and a paperback novel with its spine cracked and pages heavily annotated. He did not speak unless spoken to during the admission process. He accepted his room assignment without protest and made no request regarding room placement or roommate status.

He is physically healthy, slightly underweight for his age group, and presents no visible signs of acute distress. His affect is flat but not absent. He occasionally smiles — not at anything being said, but at some private thought he keeps entirely to himself.

Note — Ward Intake Staff

Patient placed his bag under the bed, sat at the desk, and opened his book before staff had even left the room. He did not ask when dinner was. He did not ask anything at all. The book was about birds.
