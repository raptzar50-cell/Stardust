Transcript — Verbatim

he was quite nervous, hands fidgety, shaking slightly. his eyes felt dull, stutter in voice, slow breathing — showed clear signs of anxiety.

DR. CLARK Well, it's quite a heavy rain isn't it? Feels like the city would flood. [pause] You don't talk much?
DR. CLARK Would you like some water? Make yourself comfortable.

he wasn't saying anything. only nodding.

DR. CLARK I am quite sorry we meet like this. You don't have to be scared of me — I am not an officer. I understand your situation, and I understand this is how we meet. But you must understand this is for the good of all, and I will try my best to make you feel alright during this.
DR. CLARK Shall we begin?
ALEX Y-yes.
DR. CLARK Before we begin, I want to make it clear — I'm a psychologist. But I'm not your therapist. I have been brought in by the DA to investigate what happened to your friend Sophia. I understand what you are going through and that's why we will make it easier for you. But do understand — anything you tell me isn't private the way it would be if you were seeing your own therapist. It will end up in the case file. I am not an officer. I can't arrest anyone. I can't make you say anything you don't want to say. After this is done I'll help you find a proper therapist and help in everything I can to make sure things go well for you. I'm not asking you to trust me today. I'm just asking you to know exactly what this is, so nothing about it surprises you later.
ALEX I under-stand.
DR. CLARK Before we jump into the case — you can tell me about yourself. Anything you would like.
ALEX T-thank you. I am Alex.
DR. CLARK What are you planning for ahead of you?
ALEX I am thinking to g-go for maths.
DR. CLARK That's wonderful. You know, maths — I hated it. Such a difficult subject. I'm glad you like it.
ALEX Th-ank you.
DR. CLARK So what about your family?
ALEX I ..... I live with my father.
DR. CLARK And your mom?
ALEX My parents separated.
DR. CLARK I'm sorry to hear that. It must be difficult — handling two families at once.
ALEX Y-yes.
DR. CLARK Would you like some water?
ALEX S-sure.
DR. CLARK What about your hobbies?
ALEX I like to — play guitar and I l-ike to roam.
DR. CLARK Roam? As in travel?
ALEX No — just roam, I guess.

he seems to get more comfortable.

DR. CLARK How was your freshman year going so far?
ALEX G-good. I made friends and ....
DR. CLARK And?
ALEX I — I was doing good.
DR. CLARK Glad to hear that.
DR. CLARK Have you had therapy before?
ALEX I was consulted once. I took- it.
DR. CLARK What for? If you don't mind telling?
ALEX I was suggested to have anxiety. I-I .. I took about 2 sessions.
DR. CLARK And how was it? Why did you quit?
ALEX I — I just left it out of schedule.
DR. CLARK Well — I hope I can help you even in this circumstance.
ALEX Thank you.
DR. CLARK Since you know therapy a bit, I hope it goes easy for you.
ALEX [nods]

Continuation — Dr. Clark (in-session notes only)

He opened up about the family dynamic slightly more — the father situation seems to be a significant stressor though he minimises it. The separation from the mother predates freshman year by approximately three years. He deflects when asked directly. The anxiety diagnosis from prior consultation is consistent with presentation today. I noted his breathing regulate slightly once we moved away from direct family questioning. The guitar detail is interesting — creative outlet. The "roaming" — he means walks alone, I believe. Not travel. That distinction feels important.

We did not get to Sophia today. I did not push. He needs to feel the room before I can take him anywhere near it. That was today's only goal — and he is still here, which means something.

Note: he said "I was doing good" and stopped himself. There is something in that pause. He knows what comes after "good." I'll wait for him to bring it.
